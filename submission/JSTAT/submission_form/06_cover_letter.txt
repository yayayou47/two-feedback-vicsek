To the Editorial Board Journal of Statistical Mechanics: Theory and Experiment

Dear Editors,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, ``Density-gated noise rectification in a Vicsek--Couzin flock: one dense-phase mechanism, additive at matched decorrelation,'' for publication in JSTAT as an original research article. We suggest the section Classical statistical mechanics, equilibrium and non-equilibrium; the work also touches Interdisciplinary statistical mechanics through its motivation from collective motion in living systems.

The paper makes a methodological point, and makes it by way of a concrete model. Flocking studies routinely compare noise distributions of different shape at matched nominal amplitude , but that comparison is not neutral: a wrapped -stable kick of scale decorrelates a heading at the rate = 1 - (-^), which depends on the tail index as strongly as on the amplitude. Varying the shape at fixed therefore varies the effective noise as well, and we show that this confound is large enough to manufacture an apparent cooperative effect that survives several independent consistency checks before a matched-decorrelation control dissolves it.

The vehicle is a Vicsek--Couzin flock in which one sigmoid in the local neighbour count gates both the self-propulsion speed and the stability index of the angular noise, making a crowded particle at once slow and Gaussian-like and an isolated one fast and Cauchy-like. That is deliberately the arrangement most favourable to a synergy, which is what makes a negative answer informative. A four-way ablation on a single code path, over nine sizes up to L = 256 at ten seeds, separates the channels: heavy-tailed reorientations alone grow no size-dependent susceptibility, only the motility channel builds a phase-separated dense phase, and the noise-shape channel acts exclusively inside it. Compared at each mode's own near-critical noise the full model appears to gain a super-additive boost, _9 = +0.45; under the matched-decorrelation control it falls to +0.02, additive within error, while the dense static and dynamic correlation gaps reverse sign. The coupling reduces to a density-gated lowering of the dense-phase effective noise, governed by the motility threshold alone and contingent on a metric kernel.

The components being individually well studied, we have positioned the contribution narrowly. Density-dependent motility with alignment is established, and a density-dependent noise amplitude is not new either; what has not been done is to make the shape of the turning law a function of local density and couple it to a density-dependent speed, a cell the framework of Clusella and Pastor-Satorras admits and then sets aside as beyond its scope. In the same spirit we report the finite-size slopes as effective growth rates rather than asymptotic exponents, noting that our full-mode value +1.49 is indistinguishable from the / = 1.45(2) established for the standard Vicsek model while the single-channel ablation excludes it, and that the susceptibility peak still migrates on our window. The internal falsifications are reported rather than buried.

All code and data are openly available at https://github.com/yayayou47/two-feedback-vicsek under the MIT licence, and every figure reproduces bit-for-bit from a single seeded generator. The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and its two authors declare no competing interests; one supplementary-material PDF accompanies it.

Thank you for considering our submission.
