To the Editorial Board
Journal of Statistical Mechanics: Theory and Experiment

Dear Editors,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, « Density-gated noise rectification in a Vicsek–Couzin flock: one dense-phase mechanism, additive at matched decorrelation, » for publication in JSTAT as an original research article. We suggest the section Classical statistical mechanics, equilibrium and non-equilibrium.

The paper makes a methodological point through a concrete model. Flocking studies routinely compare noise distributions of different shape at matched nominal amplitude η, but that comparison is not neutral: a wrapped α-stable kick of scale η decorrelates a heading at the rate Γ = 1 - exp(-η^α), which depends on the tail index as strongly as on the amplitude. Varying the shape at fixed η therefore varies the effective noise as well, and the confound is large enough to manufacture an apparent cooperative effect that survives several independent consistency checks before a matched-decorrelation control dissolves it.

The vehicle is a Vicsek–Couzin flock in which one sigmoid in the local neighbour count gates both the self-propulsion speed and the stability index of the angular noise, making a crowded particle slow and Gaussian-like, an isolated one fast and Cauchy-like. That is deliberately the arrangement most favourable to a synergy, which is what makes a negative answer informative. Compared at each mode's own near-critical noise the full model appears to gain a super-additive boost, Δ9 = +0.45; under the matched-decorrelation control it falls to +0.02, additive within error, while the dense correlation gaps reverse sign. The coupling reduces to a density-gated lowering of the dense-phase effective noise, contingent on a metric kernel.

We have positioned the contribution narrowly, its components being individually well studied. Density-dependent motility with alignment is established, and a density-dependent noise amplitude is not new; what has not been done is to make the shape of the turning law a function of local density and couple it to a density-dependent speed, a cell the framework of Clusella and Pastor-Satorras admits and then sets aside. In the same spirit we report the finite-size slopes as effective growth rates rather than asymptotic exponents, and the internal falsifications are reported rather than buried.

All code and data are openly available at https://github.com/yayayou47/two-feedback-vicsek under the MIT licence, and every figure reproduces bit-for-bit from a single seeded generator. The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and its two authors declare no competing interests; one supplementary-material PDF accompanies it.

Thank you for considering our submission.
