To the Editorial Board\\
\emph{Journal of Statistical Mechanics: Theory and Experiment}

\medskip

Dear Editors,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, ``Density-gated noise
rectification in a Vicsek--Couzin flock: one dense-phase
mechanism, additive at matched decorrelation,'' for
publication in JSTAT as an original research article. We suggest
the section \emph{Classical statistical mechanics, equilibrium and
non-equilibrium}; the work also touches \emph{Interdisciplinary
statistical mechanics} through its motivation from collective
motion in living systems.

The paper makes a methodological point, and makes it by way of a
concrete model. Flocking studies routinely compare noise
distributions of different shape at matched nominal amplitude
$\eta$, but that comparison is not neutral: a wrapped
$\alpha$-stable kick of scale $\eta$ decorrelates a heading at the
rate $\Gamma = 1 - \exp(-\eta^{\alpha})$, which depends on the
tail index as strongly as on the amplitude. Varying the shape at
fixed $\eta$ therefore varies the effective noise as well, and we
show that this confound is large enough to manufacture an apparent
cooperative effect that survives several independent consistency
checks before a matched-decorrelation control dissolves it.

The vehicle is a Vicsek--Couzin flock in which one sigmoid in the
local neighbour count gates both the self-propulsion speed and the
stability index of the angular noise, making a crowded particle at
once slow and Gaussian-like and an isolated one fast and
Cauchy-like. That is deliberately the arrangement most favourable
to a synergy, which is what makes a negative answer informative. A
four-way ablation on a single code path, over nine sizes up to
$L = 256$ at ten seeds, separates the channels: heavy-tailed
reorientations alone grow no size-dependent susceptibility, only
the motility channel builds a phase-separated dense phase, and the
noise-shape channel acts exclusively inside it. Compared at each
mode's own near-critical noise the full model appears to gain a
super-additive boost, $\Delta_9 = +0.45$; under the
matched-decorrelation control it falls to $+0.02$, additive within
error, while the dense static and dynamic correlation gaps reverse
sign. The coupling reduces to a density-gated lowering of the
dense-phase effective noise, governed by the motility threshold
alone and contingent on a metric kernel.

The components being individually well studied, we have positioned
the contribution narrowly. Density-dependent motility with
alignment is established, and a density-dependent noise
\emph{amplitude} is not new either; what has not been done is to
make the \emph{shape} of the turning law a function of local
density and couple it to a density-dependent speed, a cell the
framework of Clusella and Pastor-Satorras admits and then sets
aside as beyond its scope. In the same spirit we report the
finite-size slopes as effective growth rates rather than
asymptotic exponents, noting that our full-mode value $+1.49$ is
indistinguishable from the $\gamma/\nu = 1.45(2)$ established for
the standard Vicsek model while the single-channel ablation
excludes it, and that the susceptibility peak still migrates on
our window. The internal falsifications are reported rather than
buried.

All code and data are openly available at
\url{https://github.com/yayayou47/two-feedback-vicsek} under the
MIT licence, and every figure reproduces bit-for-bit from a single
seeded generator. The manuscript is original, is not under
consideration elsewhere, and its two authors declare no competing
interests; one supplementary-material PDF accompanies it.

Thank you for considering our submission.
